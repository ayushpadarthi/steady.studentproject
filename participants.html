Steady, for participants

What taking part in our drawing test would be like, what we would record, and your choices.

The study has not started. Before we test with anyone, our plan must be approved by an adult sponsor and our school's science fair review committee. Until then we are not asking anyone to take part and we are not collecting any data. The details below may change when the plan is reviewed.

What we want to find out

Steady is a drawing app that removes hand shake from the line while you draw. We want to know whether people draw more accurately with it, and whether it feels more in control, compared with no filter and with the usual kind of smoothing.

What you would do

  1. Draw on a tablet or touchscreen laptop with a stylus. The computer adds the same artificial shake to everyone's drawing (or a small motor in a special pen gently shakes the stylus), so everyone has the same shake to deal with.
  2. Three short rounds. Each round uses a different drawing setting, in a shuffled order. You won't be told which one is which.
  3. In each round: trace a spiral, draw a straight line, draw a square, then draw anything you like.
  4. After each round, answer one question: "How much control did you have?" from 0 to 10.

A session is planned to take about 20-30 minutes. You can take breaks.

What we would record

  • The position of the stylus on the screen over time, for each drawing, and your 0-10 answers.
  • A participant code (for example P07), not your name. Nothing on the files says who you are.
  • We would not record video, audio, photos or your face.

The files stay with our team and are used only for this project. Results are shown as averages and graphs, never as one person's drawing with a name.

Your choices

  • Taking part is voluntary. Saying no is completely fine.
  • You can stop at any time, without giving a reason, and ask for your drawings to be deleted.
  • If you are under 18, a parent or guardian would also need to agree.

Safety

  • The shaking pen moves only a few millimetres, is limited in software to a small angle, and switches itself off after 15 seconds. You can simply let go of it.
  • Drawing on a screen is an everyday activity; we expect no risk beyond normal tablet use.
  • This is not a medical test. It does not diagnose or treat anything, and we cannot give medical advice. Steady only changes what appears on the screen.

Questions

Ask any of the student researchers, or our teacher sponsor, in person. You can try the app yourself any time: nothing you draw at home leaves your device.
